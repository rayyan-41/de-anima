Literature, at its most profound, is not merely a mirror held up to life but a scalpel that dissects the human soul, exposing the raw nerves of existence. One of its most enduring ideas is the tension between fate and free will—a paradox that forces characters and readers alike to confront the illusion of control. Great works do not resolve this tension but instead magnify it, revealing how individuals cling to agency even in the face of inevitability. Whether through the tragic downfall of a king, the quiet despair of a trapped housewife, or the cosmic indifference of a universe that cares nothing for human striving, literature lays bare the fragile boundaries between choice and destiny. In this struggle, we see ourselves: creatures who act as if we are free, even when the weight of history, biology, and chance presses down upon us.
