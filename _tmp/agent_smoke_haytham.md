What if the act of seeing—something we do effortlessly every waking moment—is not a passive reception of the world, but an active, almost alchemical transformation of light into meaning? The eye does not merely collect rays; it *interprets* them. Light enters the cornea, bends, focuses, and strikes the retina, where millions of photoreceptors convert photons into electrical signals. Yet this is only the beginning. Those signals travel through a labyrinth of neural circuits, where the brain does not reconstruct an image like a camera, but *infers* a world—filling gaps, correcting distortions, and stitching together fragments into a seamless whole. The question is not just how we see, but why we see *what we expect to see*. What happens when the brain’s predictions clash with reality? That tension is where perception becomes not just biology, but a dialogue between the mind and the universe.
