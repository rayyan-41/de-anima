One of the most profound yet misunderstood principles in Islam is the distinction between *taqlid* (blind imitation) and *ittiba’* (informed adherence). While *taqlid* chains a believer to the rulings of a scholar without understanding the evidence, *ittiba’* demands that one follow the Quran and Sunnah directly, with full awareness of the reasoning behind a ruling. **Imam Al-Ghazali** warned against the dangers of uncritical imitation, arguing that true faith requires intellectual engagement—submission to Allah must be rooted in knowledge, not habit or cultural inertia. The Quran itself commands, *"Ask those who know if you do not know"* (16:43), but this is not a license for blind obedience; it is an invitation to seek evidence, question assumptions, and align one’s practice with the Prophetic model. The tragedy of our time is that many Muslims confuse cultural traditions with divine command, mistaking inherited customs for *deen*. True devotion lies not in repeating the past, but in understanding why the past was right—and where it strayed.
