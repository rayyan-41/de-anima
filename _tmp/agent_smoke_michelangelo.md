The Renaissance concept of *disegno*—the Italian term for drawing or design—was far more than a mere technical skill; it was the intellectual foundation of all artistic creation. To masters like Michelangelo and Leonardo da Vinci, *disegno* represented the artist’s ability to conceive and structure an idea before committing it to material form. It was the bridge between imagination and execution, where composition, proportion, and narrative were first resolved in the mind and on paper. This principle elevated art from craft to philosophy, insisting that true mastery began not with the brush or chisel, but with the disciplined act of thinking. In this way, *disegno* became the silent revolution that transformed artists into thinkers, and workshops into laboratories of human expression.
